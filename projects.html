Ideas in the game

Six design ideas Air Trapeze is built on — each one small enough to say in a sentence, each one doing real work in the game.

  1. Three-Act Contracts

    Every contract is split into three acts, and life events land between them: rent, a debt collector, a layoff. Only trades worth at least 1% of your starting equity count toward an act, so a $10 scalp cannot skip the story.

    • pacing
    • goals
    • scoring

  2. Fat Fish Credit

    A loan shark waits in the corner of the screen. The money keeps the position alive and makes the contract harder at the same time, because the debt becomes a goal you have to clear before the run is over.

    • debt
    • economy
    • narrative

  3. Ten-Second Emergency

    When news breaks while a position is open, a countdown covers the screen and the market keeps moving underneath it. Do nothing and the game assumes you held — two of those per contract, no more.

    • real-time
    • pressure
    • risk

  4. Strategy Cards

    Four schools — trend, news, contrarian, defence — three tiers each, three slots on the desk. Cards are bought between acts, upgraded by buying the same card again, and sold back for half.

    • deck-building
    • progression

  5. Interest, Paid in Cash

    At every act break your cash pays 5%, capped at $500 — but only the cash you did not spend on a card. Buy now to survive the next act, or bank it and go for the expensive card later.

    • trade-off
    • economy

  6. The Medical Record

    The game keeps a file on you: every run, every bail-out, every wipe-out, with the numbers behind it. It is the one part of the game that gets more useful the worse you play.

    • persistence
    • stats
    • storytelling

All six are live in Air Trapeze — the game is the proof of concept, not the plan.
